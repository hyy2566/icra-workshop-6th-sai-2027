01 / The shared question

Better assistance.
Meaningful human control.

Adaptive assistance brings together sensing, robot control and human judgment. This edition focuses on how to evaluate the decisions between them in contact-rich teleoperation and physical human–robot interaction.

How should we evaluate assistance when model predictions, physical outcomes and the operator’s experience do not align?

Shared autonomyDigital twinsHuman agencyTeleoperationPhysical HRI
  1. 01
    Recognise uncertainty.

    Separate degraded sensing, model mismatch and uncertainty about human intent.

  2. 02
    Make assistance understandable.

    Define when to intervene, request clarification or return control, and what feedback the operator needs.

  3. 03
    Connect claims with evidence.

    Evaluate physical outcomes alongside agency, understanding and workload, using appropriate evidence for each.

04 / Work through a shared scenario

Act, ask,
or return control?

A facilitated discussion will bring researchers with different expertise together around the same situation. No specialised hardware or programming experience is required.

An illustrative situation

A person is remotely guiding a robot through a contact task. The view becomes occluded. A digital twin predicts a safe contact, but the observed result differs. The robot suggests continuing; the operator asks to pause.

What should happen next? Specify the decision, its trigger, the operator’s feedback and override options, and the evidence needed to evaluate it.
  1. 90MINUTES
    Explore together

    Mixed-expertise groups work through sensing degradation, model mismatch and assistance decisions.

  2. 35MINUTES
    Compare the reasoning

    Share assumptions and critique what evidence each proposed decision would require.

  3. 45MINUTES
    Draft an evaluation framework

    Collect intervention rules, feedback, baselines, physical measures and human measures, preserving open questions.

Participants may contribute by speaking, writing or completing a worksheet. The exercise compares evaluation plans; empirical claims about task performance or workload require subsequent experimental evidence.

05 / Contributions

Bring your methods.
Bring your open questions.

Planned contribution formats welcome empirical studies, methods, negative results, replication, work in progress and application case studies across robotics, control, HCI and human factors.

Research & work in progress

2–4 page papers

Nonarchival contributions describing an evaluation claim, its evidence, limitations and connection to the workshop questions.

Systems & demonstrations

1 page demo proposals

Demonstrations may use video, so participation need not depend on transporting equipment.

The proposed review process uses two reviewers per submission, considering relevance, methodological clarity and the match between claims and evidence. The planned capacity is 24 contributions, including up to four demos, with up to twelve short spotlights. Accepted contributions will have poster or demo space.

Researchers at all career stages and people new to teleoperation are welcome. General attendance will not require an accepted paper; conference registration requirements will apply.
